Prepared by Fruitbowl. Research/discovery phase only — no design or build decisions made.

*This is a living discovery document. It has been revised twice: Pass 2 deepened the public social/

## 1. Executive Summary

Faded Barbers Selby is a young (incorporated Aug 2024), well-reviewed, two-owner barbershop
competing in a crowded Selby market (10+ barbers found in search). Its digital presence today is a
Squire booking page and an Instagram account (@fadedselby, 476 followers, 29 posts) — there is no
dedicated website, and no confirmed Facebook or TikTok presence. Reviews are strong (5.0★ wherever
found) but reported separately by Google and Squire with different counts — a labelling problem,
not a rating problem, and one this report is careful not to paper over by inventing a single
"combined" number.

Having now seen actual Instagram content directly (not just search snippets), the headline finding
has changed since Pass 1: **Faded already has a real, consistent brand system** — a distinct
black/white/grey grain-textured visual identity, a boxed "FADED" wordmark, a confident and
culturally-plugged-in tone of voice, and team-led content built around two named co-owners, Elliot
and Bailey. What Faded doesn't have is anywhere to put that identity to work beyond a single social
feed and a generic third-party booking template. Squire is quietly doing jobs it wasn't designed
for — first impression, service info, and booking all at once — while the actual brand lives almost
entirely on Instagram, where it's real but capped by the format (one link, one feed, no structure).

We still do **not** think the first move is "build Faded a website" reflexively — see §9 for the
case, and the explicit split between what Faded should prioritise as a client versus what this
speculative prototype can responsibly demonstrate without access to Faded's own accounts.

## 3. What Faded Is Already Doing Well

- **A real, consistent visual and verbal brand already exists.** This is the single biggest update
  since Pass 1/2: Faded is not starting from nothing on brand — see §7 and §12 for the evidence.
  This changes the shape of any future recommendation from "build a brand" to "give an existing
  brand somewhere better to live."
- **Reviews are genuinely strong** — 5.0★ wherever found, with review text describing consistency
  and atmosphere repeatedly, not just one good visit.
- **Booking already works** — Squire is a legitimate, low-friction booking tool, and the Instagram
  bio link correctly points straight at it. They are not starting from "no online booking" or "a
  buried, wrong link," which are the two most common failure points for local service businesses.
- **They already repackage reviews as on-brand social content** — a designed, on-brand
  review-graphic post (logo, star rating, pull quote) exists in the grid. This corrects Pass 1's
  "no obvious review strategy" framing: there is a review-amplification habit, it just isn't yet
  extended into growing the underlying Google review volume.
- **Team-led, personality-forward content is already a deliberate strategy**, not an accident —
  "meet the faces behind the fades," named co-owner profiles, team-join announcements, and personal
  `fadedby[name]` accounts all point the same way.
- **A consistent, always-on acquisition offer exists (FADED25)**, applied via a single code on a
  single platform (Squire) across at least five posts spanning roughly six months — a real,
  repeated mechanism, not a one-off (see §11 for the eligibility-wording caveat).
- **Clear, simple pricing** and **realistic trade hours** (Thu/Fri lates, early Saturday) — both
  unchanged from Pass 1 and still genuine strengths.

## 4. Current Weaknesses / Friction

- **No owned digital home.** Everything — booking, brand, identity — lives on platforms Faded
  doesn't control the structure of. The brand shown in §3/§7 is real, but it's confined to a single
  Instagram feed and a generic Squire template; there's no page that's "just Faded's," built to
  Faded's own structure.
- **Social proof is fragmented across platforms with different counts** — a labelling/consistency
  problem, not a lack of proof. See §11 for how to fix this without creating a misleading number.
- **The FADED25 offer's eligibility wording is genuinely inconsistent across posts** — some state
  it's for new/first-time clients only, one explicitly states it covers existing clients too (see
  §11 for the full breakdown). This is a real, evidenced friction point: a promo code whose terms
  visibly change between posts is confusing at best and creates redemption disputes at worst.
- **Instagram content is currently graphic-design-led, not photography/video-led.** Of the visible
  grid content, the large majority are designed text-on-background promo/info graphics rather than
  raw photography of cuts, the shop, or clients. For a highly visual trade like barbering, this is
  a real, specific gap — not "they need more content," but "they need more of the *photographic*
  kind of content specifically" (see §12).
- **No presence on Facebook or TikTok found**, both of which materially help local-business
  discovery and (for TikTok specifically) short-form transformation content.
- **No dedicated website means no local SEO surface area** independent of third-party platforms.
- **A calmer, "downtime" brand theme ("Step away from the noise...") appears only once** against a
  majority of punchier, CTA-driven posts — see §7. Not a weakness exactly, but worth naming: it's
  an underdeveloped thread, not yet a consistent pillar.

## 5. Customer Journey Audit

Walking the journey as a prospective Selby customer:

**Discovery** — A search for "barber Selby" surfaces a crowded field (10+ shops). Faded appears via
directory listings and its Squire page, competing against Blak Bear's dedicated website and several
other established shops. Nothing at this exact moment (search results, directory listings) carries
any of Faded's actual brand personality — that only becomes visible once someone specifically finds
and opens the Instagram account.

**Trust** — On Faded's Squire page: hours, price, and (per Squire's own count) reviews. What's
missing there specifically: team identity, shop atmosphere, and the visual brand — all of which
*do* exist, just one click away on Instagram rather than integrated into the platform doing the
"convince me" job.

**Consideration** — Now confirmed, not assumed: Instagram *is* carrying real brand weight — a
consistent visual system, named co-owners, an offer, reviews repackaged as content. The gap isn't
"is there anything here" (Pass 2's open question) — it's that a single feed, one link, and
graphic-heavy content can't do everything a considering customer might want (see specific team
member's work, book a specific barber, see the space, watch a cut happen).

**Booking** — Squire itself is low-friction and correctly linked from Instagram — a genuine
strength.

**Visit** — No direct evidence of the in-shop experience beyond review text and photography, both
positive.

**Repeat customer / review / referral** — Still no evidence of a structured post-visit flow beyond
organic reviews and the existing review-graphic content habit. The FADED25 mechanism is oriented
toward new-client acquisition in most of its instances (see §11) rather than repeat-visit or
referral behaviour specifically — a gap in the *retention* half of the funnel that the acquisition
side doesn't address.

**Overall read:** Squire prevents a catastrophic gap, and Faded's Instagram is doing more real
brand work than Pass 1 gave it credit for. The weakness is structural, not creative: a real brand
with nowhere but a single social feed to fully express itself, and no retention/referral mechanism
to match the acquisition one.

## 6. Competitor / Benchmark Findings

**Blak Bear (Selby, York x2, Harrogate)** — the clearest local benchmark:
- Multi-site scale and a 2018 founding date give them a six-year head start on brand and review
  accumulation that Faded (est. 2024) cannot shortcut.
- Has both a dedicated website *and* Squire booking — the site is the brand front-end, Squire does
  the actual booking. This validates the "don't replace Squire" hypothesis.
- Runs an explicit acquisition offer (20% off first cut via a code), applied consistently across
  channels.
- Also runs a recurring "Meet the [City] team" Instagram format introducing individual barbers by
  name and years of experience — a direct structural parallel to Faded's own "meet the faces behind
  the fades" content. (Correction from an earlier research pass: this format was initially
  misattributed to Faded by an automated search summary; verified against the source URLs, it
  belongs to Blak Bear's account, `@blak_bear_barbering`.) Worth naming because it confirms
  team-led content is a proven pattern in this specific local market, not a Faded-only idea.
- Broader channel presence: Facebook plus Instagram, alongside directory and booking-platform
  listings.

**What to take from this, without copying it:** the pattern worth adopting is *structure* (owned
site + third-party booking, a real offer mechanism, team-led content) — not Blak Bear's scale or
visual identity. Faded already has its own distinct visual and verbal identity (§7); the
opportunity is to give that more room, not to imitate Blak Bear's.

**Wider Selby market:** at least eight other named competitors surfaced in search ("BarberLou,"
"Lucy Jaynes Barbershop," "Illicit Studios," "Top Cut Barber," "High Seas Barbershop," "Stag and
Boy Barbershop," "Tracey's Barbers," "Istanbul Barbers Selby"). Selby is not an underserved market —
differentiation matters more than "just having a website" on its own.

## 7. Brand Conclusions

*Based on direct evidence from user-supplied screenshots of Faded's own Instagram
(`docs/research/instagram/`), cross-checked against independent web research from Passes 1–2.*

**Visual language (observation, drawn directly from the screenshots):**
- A restrained, near-monochrome palette — black, white, grey — used consistently across every post
  seen.
- A recurring **film-grain/noise texture** overlaid on both photography and graphic posts — the
  single most consistent visual "signature" found.
- A boxed **"FADED" wordmark** (bold, condensed sans-serif, all-caps, inside a thin-line rectangle)
  with "BARBERS" tracked out in small type beneath it — used as a repeated logo lockup across
  multiple post types.
- Bold, large-scale, condensed sans-serif headline type carries most posts — text-on-image is the
  dominant format, not photography with light captioning.
- Team/portrait photography uses a consistent **grey seamless studio backdrop**, shot mid-cut,
  with the subject's name and role overlaid in clean white type — a repeatable, professional
  template, not one-off phone snaps.
- Shop/product photography (e.g. the shopfront shot) is dark, moody, high-contrast — consistent
  with the grain-and-monochrome system rather than bright, traditional barbershop imagery.

**Inference (interpretation drawn from the above, not stated anywhere directly):** the visual
system reads as closer to **streetwear/editorial** than to traditional gentleman's-barber styling —
dark studio lighting, grain, bold condensed type, and plain black clothing on subjects all point
the same direction. This is a specific, evidenced position, not a generic "modern barber" label.

**Tone of voice (observation, using verbatim copy from the screenshots):**
- Confident, direct, CTA-forward: almost every caption ends in a booking prompt ("Hit the link in
  our bio," "Book in with the team via Squire," "Don't forget to book in using link in our bio").
- Playful and internet-native: a fake iPhone "Do Not Disturb / Personal / Trim Time" notification
  graphic; a "Weekend is loading..." spinner graphic; a listicle hook ("5 signs it's time for a
  haircut. Don't ignore number 5...").
- Culturally plugged-in and locally toned: "IT'S COMING HOME. SO YOUR TRIM BETTER BE TOO" — a
  football-culture reference ("football's coming home") repurposed as a haircut pun. Reads as
  British, laddish-but-warm, in on the joke rather than corporate.
- Inclusive and casual: "New here or a regular in the chair — we've got you covered," "Guess who's
  back," referring to the team as "the FADED family."
- One clear outlier: "Step away from the noise. Sit back, switch off and let us take care of the
  rest." — calmer, more restrained, spa-like language. This appears once in the material reviewed,
  against a clear majority of punchier, CTA-driven, meme-format posts.

**Inference:** the dominant tone is confident, playful, and sales-forward but executed with real
style rather than hard-sell cheesiness — closer to "a good mate who happens to be excellent at his
job" than either a traditional barbershop or a hushed premium grooming lounge.

**Brand personality (inference, synthesising visual + verbal evidence):** team-led, locally
rooted, confident, culturally current, and design-conscious. Not fashion-editorial in a
disconnected, aspirational way — the streetwear/editorial visual cues are grounded by very local,
very specific humour (a football reference, "the FADED family," named team members with regular
Selby clients leaving specific, personal reviews). Not a generic "friendly neighbourhood barber"
label — the evidence supports something more specific than that.

**The "step away from the noise" theme — explicitly assessed, per instruction, rather than
over-elevated:** on the evidence available (one post out of the material reviewed), this reads as
a **creative opportunity / emerging theme**, not a confirmed brand pillar. It's a genuinely
interesting idea — the haircut as a moment of deliberate downtime — and it doesn't contradict
anything else in the brand, but it hasn't yet been repeated or built out into a recurring
proposition. Worth testing further, not assuming as a settled positioning pillar.

**Team-led brand (observation + inference):** the evidence — named co-owners, "meet the faces
behind the fades," personal `fadedby[name]` accounts, public team-join announcements — supports
personality as a deliberate, real part of how Faded already presents itself, not something that
would need to be invented. Whether individual barbers have their own following large enough to
independently drive bookings is not evidenced either way (Elliot's and Will's personal accounts
exist; their reach is unknown — open question, §16).

**What should NOT be lost when modernising:**
- The grain/monochrome visual system — it's distinctive and consistently applied; a generic clean
  "premium web agency" aesthetic would actively erase a working brand asset.
- The specific, local, slightly cheeky tone — replacing it with polished-corporate copy would flatten
  the most human, differentiating thing Faded currently has.
- Named-owner, team-first framing — "meet the faces behind the fades" is doing real work; it
  shouldn't become generic "our team" boilerplate.

**Where the identity currently feels inconsistent or underdeveloped:**
- The offer's terms (§11).
- The "downtime" theme is present but thin — one execution, not a system.
- The account leans heavily on graphic-design posts over photography/video of actual work — for a
  visual trade, the strongest possible proof (the cuts themselves, in motion, in the chair) is
  currently underrepresented relative to promotional graphics.

**If a Faded prototype had no logo on it, what would still need to be true for it to feel
recognisably Faded, not a generic barber?** On the evidence gathered: it would need the
grain-textured black/white/grey visual treatment (not a clean, light, "premium neutral" palette);
bold, confident, condensed display type doing a lot of the talking; real named team members shown
doing real work, framed personally rather than corporately; copy with genuine wit and local/cultural
specificity rather than generic "quality haircuts" language; and a direct, unapologetic push toward
booking rather than a soft, browse-first experience. A prototype that swapped in soft pastels,
generic stock-style photography, or corporate-safe copy would not read as Faded even with the name
attached.

## 8. Fruitbowl Discovery-Method Note

**Social-first brand discovery.** For any business with an existing social presence, treat their
own social channels as primary research material before designing anything — they usually hold the
most authentic evidence of personality, team, tone, visual language, real work, and community that
exists anywhere about the business. This project is the working example: Pass 1/2's arm's-length
web research established the factual skeleton (registration, hours, reviews, competitors) but could
only speculate about brand; direct screenshots of eight Instagram posts changed the entire framing
of this report from "Faded needs a brand" to "Faded has a brand that needs somewhere better to
live."

**Practical method, as used here:**
1. Establish the factual skeleton first via public records and search (registration, address,
   hours, reviews, competitors) — cheap, verifiable, low-risk.
2. Attempt legitimate indirect routes into social content (indexed captions, cross-referencing
   public registers, following name/handle trails) — sometimes enough, sometimes not; document what
   was tried and what it returned either way, rather than stopping at "inaccessible."
3. Where indirect access is genuinely insufficient, ask the client (or, as here, the internal
   sponsor) for direct screenshots/exports rather than guessing or inventing placeholder brand
   direction.
4. When primary evidence arrives, treat it as authoritative over earlier inference, but keep the
   provenance trail visible — what's a direct quote/observation versus an interpretation built on
   top of it.
5. The goal is never to reproduce the social feed as a website. It's to extract the *repeatable
   signals* (palette, texture, type, tone, recurring phrases, team-led framing) that make the brand
   recognisable, and carry those into a stronger, better-structured digital presence — preserving
   what's authentic rather than replacing it with a generic "modern local business" look.

## 9. Website Opportunity

**Would a dedicated website materially help? Conditionally yes — but not as a Squire replacement,
and not as the first thing built reflexively.**

The case *for*: nothing Faded owns today can rank in local search independently, or give its
existing brand (§7) a structured home beyond a single feed. A website is the only channel here
Faded would fully control the structure, SEO, and content of — and, now that we can see the actual
brand, there's real, distinctive material to build it around rather than inventing one from
scratch.

The case *against jumping straight there*: Squire already solves booking adequately — rebuilding
that is wasted effort and risk. And the two areas most worth expanding — real photography/video of
the actual work (§12) and a resolved, consistent offer (§11) — are cheaper, faster wins than a full
site build, and would make any future site materially stronger when it does happen.

**An important distinction the previous pass blurred:**
- **What Faded should prioritise once/if they become a Fruitbowl client** — likely GBP
  optimisation, offer/terms consistency, and photography/video investment, ahead of a full site
  build (see §15).
- **What Operation Faded can responsibly prioritise right now, as a speculative Fruitbowl
  prototype** — we do not control Faded's Google Business Profile, Squire account, or operating
  systems, and shouldn't imply otherwise. What a prototype *can* responsibly do is demonstrate, in
  a self-contained way, what a premium front-end built around Faded's own existing brand (§7) could
  look like — using only publicly available material and the visual/verbal language already
  evidenced here — as a proof of value, not a live operational fix.

**Recommended role for a website, if commissioned:** a premium front-end that carries Faded's own
existing visual/verbal identity (not a new one), gives the team-led content (§7) a permanent home
beyond a single feed, surfaces services/pricing clearly, funnels every booking intent straight into
Squire, and presents reviews from each platform separately and transparently (§11) — not a Squire
replacement, an addition to the funnel.

## 10. Local SEO / Discoverability Opportunity

- **Google Business Profile is almost certainly the single highest-leverage lever available for
  Faded as an operating business.** We don't know its current claim/verification/completeness
  status — genuinely unknown, not something a prototype can fix without account access (see §9's
  distinction).
- Presenting reviews transparently by platform (§11) still feeds trust and, indirectly, local
  search performance.
- A dedicated website, if built, adds real local SEO surface area Squire/Instagram can't: a page
  targeting Selby + service terms, structured data (LocalBusiness schema), and content that can
  earn local backlinks/citations.
- Directory consistency (NAP — name, address, phone — across Squire, Yorkshire.com, Yell, GBP, any
  future site) is a basic, frequently-missed win worth auditing rather than assuming.
- On AI/LLM discovery: worth being aware of, not worth over-investing in. The same underlying
  signals (GBP data, review volume/recency, structured citations) feed both traditional and AI
  answer engines — get the fundamentals right rather than chasing bespoke "AI SEO."

## 11. Reviews and Promotions

**Reviews — how to present them (revised per explicit instruction not to combine platforms):**
Faded's reviews should always be shown **separately and clearly labelled by source** — e.g. "Google:
5.0★ (23 reviews)" and "Squire: 5.0★ (85 reviews)" shown side by side or in clearly separate blocks
— never merged into one implied "108 reviews" or similar. Where a total across platforms is shown
at all, it should only be a simple, clearly-labelled sum ("108 reviews across Google and Squire"),
never presented as a single unified rating, since the two platforms' rating calculations aren't
directly comparable.

Faded already has a review-amplification habit — a designed on-brand social graphic quoting a real
5-star review, in the same visual system as everything else (§7). The gap isn't strategy, it's
volume: growing the underlying Google review count (still legitimate, non-incentivised methods —
a simple, consistently-timed ask right after a good visit, a single-tap direct GBP review link)
would give that existing content habit more to work with.

**Promotions — FADED25, now analysed from real posts rather than an unverified claim:**
Confirmed mechanism: a single code, **FADED25**, redeemed through Squire, appearing across at least
five Instagram posts from roughly January through July. This is a real, repeated, consistently
-executed acquisition mechanism — stronger evidence of intent than Pass 1/2 could establish.

**Documented discrepancy in eligibility wording (not resolved — presented as found):**
- One post (3 January): explicitly states the offer is open to **both new and existing clients**
  ("New to the shop? We've got you covered. Existing Clients? We've also got you covered.").
- Multiple later posts (27 May, 1 June, 23 June): each specify **"first trim" / "new clients" /
  "first time customers" only**.

Read chronologically, this looks like the offer may have narrowed from an all-clients promotion to
a new-client-acquisition-only one — but it's equally possible this is inconsistent copywriting
across posts rather than a deliberate change, and the material available doesn't let us distinguish
the two. **This should be verified directly with Faded before any funnel work assumes one version
is the current, correct terms** — redeeming a code under terms the shop no longer honours is a real
customer-experience and trust risk.

## 12. Content / Social Opportunity

**Confirmed content mix (from the screenshot grid):** promotional/booking-CTA graphics, a
services-list graphic paired with a work-in-progress photo, a listicle-style hook post ("5 signs
it's time for a haircut"), a culturally-topical pun graphic (the football reference), team-intro
carousels, and a designed review-quote graphic. The clear majority of visible tiles are **designed
graphics with text overlays**, not raw photography or video of cuts, clients, or the shop.

**The specific, evidenced opportunity this points to:** not "Faded needs more content" in general —
they post consistently and the brand system is strong — but specifically **more real photography
and video of the actual work**, in the same grain/monochrome visual language already established.
Short-form vertical video (a fade being cut, a finished-cut reveal, shop atmosphere) is a natural
fit for barbering and is currently the least-represented format in what's visible, despite being
arguably the strongest possible proof of skill.

- Team/barber personality content is already a demonstrated strength (§7) — more of the same
  format (named, titled, shot against the consistent studio backdrop) for the rest of the team
  (Jake, Josh, Will, and any others) would extend a working pattern rather than invent a new one.
- This remains the clearest area where **Fruitbowl's combined build + visual content offering** is
  a genuine differentiator: Faded already has the creative direction figured out (§7) — what's
  missing is the photographic/video execution of it, which is exactly where a build-plus-content
  agency adds value a pure web agency or a pure content creator each can't alone.

## 13. Business / Automation Opportunities

Kept intentionally modest — no tech-for-tech's-sake recommendations:
- **Offer-terms consistency**: a single, current, unambiguous statement of FADED25's terms used
  everywhere it's referenced — directly serves §11, very low effort, addresses a real evidenced
  problem rather than a hypothetical one.
- **Review-request automation**: a simple, compliant post-visit trigger (SMS/email/QR at checkout)
  to grow Google review volume behind the existing amplification habit — directly serves §11.
- **Offer tracking**: a single trackable link/code so FADED25's actual performance is visible, not
  just assumed.
- **Booking-source attribution**: knowing whether a booking came from Instagram, Google, or a
  future website is cheap to set up and currently appears not to exist — a measurement gap that
  makes every other recommendation here provable or disprovable later.
- Anything more elaborate (CRM, loyalty apps, chatbots) is not justified by anything found in this
  research.
