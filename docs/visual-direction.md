# Operation Faded — Visual Direction + Information Architecture

Prepared by Fruitbowl. This is a decision document, not build output. It translates
`docs/discovery.md` and `docs/brief.md` into concrete visual/structural direction: three credible
creative directions, one decisive recommendation, and a homepage narrative detailed enough to hand
to build with confidence.

## 1. Constraints Carried From Discovery + Brief

Non-negotiable, sourced back to specific evidence — every direction below has to satisfy all of
these:

- **Monochrome, grain-textured visual system** — black/white/grey, consistent film-grain overlay,
  the single most consistent visual signature found (discovery §7).
- **Bold condensed typography**, used flexibly (all-caps and sentence-case both appear — not one
  rigid rule) — discovery §7, confirmed on re-inspection.
- **The boxed "FADED." wordmark** (with its trailing period), "BARBERS" tracked out beneath —
  reused directly wherever possible; a faithful temporary derived version may be used only when
  necessary for this prototype, clearly labelled internally and never presented as Faded's official
  source logo (discovery §7, brief §9, §16).
- **Team-led identity** — Elliot and Bailey named as Co-Owners, "meet the faces behind the fades"
  as proven framing, not invented (discovery §7).
- **Local, culturally-current tone** — confident, playful, specific (the football pun, the
  meme-format posts), never generic "quality haircuts" copy (discovery §7).
- **Real imagery and real brand assets first** — recognisability must come from Faded's actual
  identity, not a reproduced palette (brief §9).
- **Squire remains the booking engine** — no parallel booking system, ever (discovery §9, brief §7).
- **Booking is the primary action, but not the only purpose** — the page also has to elevate brand
  and build trust/consideration (brief §4).
- **Reviews shown honestly, never combined into one fabricated number** (discovery §11, brief §5).
- **FADED25 shown prominently but with neutral/placeholder terms** — discovery found conflicting
  eligibility wording; the brief explicitly declines to resolve it on Faded's behalf (brief §8).
- **Must feel recognisably Faded, not generically "premium"** — the 15-second test is the standing
  bar for every decision (brief §3–4).
- **Clear places for future Fruitbowl-shot photography/video** to elevate the prototype later,
  without a redesign (brief §9).

## 2. Creative Direction A — "Studio Cut"

**Concept.** An editorial, documentary-photography-led homepage: the site behaves like a quiet,
confident magazine feature on the shop, not a poster wall. Grain is atmosphere, not decoration.

**Brand expression.** Monochrome and grain carried through as *photographic* qualities (film-like
tonality) rather than graphic overlays. The wordmark appears sparingly — once in the header, once
in the hero — rather than repeated as a graphic device throughout. Typography stays restrained and
secondary to imagery; team/humour/booking language appears as short, quiet captions rather than
bold poster headlines. Editorial/streetwear cues come through in photography styling (the grey
seamless backdrop, plain black clothing) rather than in graphic layout.

**Emotional feel.** Calm confidence. Like being shown around by someone who doesn't need to raise
their voice to prove they're good at the job.

**First-screen experience.** A large, full-bleed real photograph (shopfront or a portrait),
minimal text — a short line, the wordmark, one CTA — lots of negative space.

**Photography/video treatment.** Everything hinges on photography quality and volume, which is
currently the thinnest part of the evidence base (one shopfront shot, two portraits). Real assets
carry real weight here; future Fruitbowl photography would elevate this direction dramatically —
but in its v1 form, with only three real images to draw on, large full-bleed treatment would expose
the gap rather than disguise it.

**Motion/interaction.** Slow, subtle image crossfades between sections; minimal else — the
direction's whole point is restraint.

**Risks.** With today's asset library, this direction is the hardest to execute convincingly
without leaning on placeholders more than any of the three — directly in tension with the brief's
"recognisable via real assets" requirement. Also risks reading as more "premium web agency
portfolio" than "real Selby barbershop with personality" if the copy/humour gets dialled down too
far in service of restraint.

## 3. Creative Direction B — "Fresh Cut Graphics"

**Concept.** A bold, graphic-poster-led homepage that leans hardest into what Faded already does
constantly on Instagram: large condensed type, the boxed wordmark, high-contrast text-on-dark
blocks, occasional painted-texture accents.

**Brand expression.** The graphic system *is* the homepage — wordmark, grain, and bold type appear
throughout, not just in the hero. The pipe-separated services list style, the "5 signs…" listicle
hook format, and the brush-texture cross device all get reused directly as homepage devices.
Photography plays a supporting role inside graphic compositions rather than carrying full-bleed
sections. Booking language stays punchy and CTA-forward, matching the Instagram captions closely.

**Emotional feel.** Energetic, confident, unmistakably online-native — the digital equivalent of
walking past a shop with great signage and knowing exactly what it is before you're even at the
door.

**First-screen experience.** Bold wordmark treatment, a short punchy line in Faded's own voice,
graphic texture/grain as a full background, CTA styled as boldly as the rest of the page.

**Photography/video treatment.** Real photography is used in smaller, framed placements (inside
graphic compositions, as accents) rather than full-bleed heroes — genuinely well-suited to today's
thin photography library, and would slot in more real photography over time as accent imagery
without needing to be full-bleed-quality from day one.

**Motion/interaction.** Type-in/reveal animations on headlines, grain texture subtly animating in
the background, snappy section transitions matching the energetic tone.

**Risks.** The clearest risk of all three: this is the direction closest to literally restating the
Instagram grid as a webpage, which discovery (§8) explicitly frames as *not* the goal — the point
of a website is to give the brand somewhere to do more than the feed already does, not to
re-present the same graphics in a browser. Also risks feeling repetitive or shouty if every section
uses the same bold-graphic device without variation.

## 4. Creative Direction C — "Faces Behind the Fades" — Recommended

**Concept.** A team-led editorial homepage built around Faded's single strongest, most distinctive,
already-proven real asset: the co-owner portrait format (grey seamless backdrop, grain, name+title
overlay). The graphic system supports this rather than competing with it.

**Brand expression.** Elliot and Bailey's portraits, in their existing proven style, are the
visual anchor of the page — not a supporting element. The boxed wordmark, bold condensed
typography, and grain treatment wrap around and frame the team content rather than dominating
independently of it. The services list keeps its authentic pipe-separated typographic treatment.
Copy stays personal and specific — "Meet the faces behind the fades" literally becomes the
homepage's second act, not a single Instagram post. Humour and local/cultural tone show up in
supporting copy (captions, microcopy) rather than being the headline device.

**Emotional feel.** Trust through people. Like being introduced to the two people who'll actually
be cutting your hair, by name, before you've even opened the booking page.

**First-screen experience.** Hero establishes the brand system fast (wordmark, grain, one line, CTA)
and then, within the first scroll or two, the visitor is looking at Elliot and Bailey by name — the
fastest possible route through discovery's own "understand" test.

**Photography/video treatment.** Uses exactly what's strongest today — the two real portraits — at
real, confident scale, rather than stretching thin photography across full-bleed sections it can't
support (Direction A's risk) or shrinking it into accents (Direction B's approach). Structured so
that adding Jake, Josh, and Will in the same portrait format is a direct extension, not a
redesign — and so that future Fruitbowl-shot video (a barber talking briefly to camera, a cut in
progress) drops directly into the same team section without restructuring anything.

**Motion/interaction.** A subtle grain-to-clear reveal as each portrait enters the viewport on
scroll (thematically apt for a brand literally called "Faded," without being a gimmick); gentle
hover states on team cards (slight scale/grain shift); otherwise restrained, in service of the
people being looked at rather than the interface showing off.

**Risks.** Depends more than the other directions on the two existing portraits carrying real
visual weight — if reproduced at low resolution (they're currently screenshot-derived), quality
could disappoint on a large hero-scale treatment; this needs to be checked in build (see §16) and
is the strongest single argument for prioritising a real photography top-up before this goes fully
live. Also risks under-representing the graphic/meme personality that makes Faded's *voice* so
distinct if the team focus isn't balanced with enough of that copy tone elsewhere on the page.

| Criterion (brief §3) | A — Studio Cut | B — Fresh Cut Graphics | C — Faces Behind the Fades |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognisable via real identity/imagery | Weak today (thin photography) | Strong (matches IG closely) | **Strongest** (uses the single best real asset at full weight) |
| Credible/premium vs. current Squire page | Strong if assets existed | Good | **Strong**, and credible in a way graphics alone can't be — real people |
| Easy to understand who/what/where fast | Moderate (relies on captions) | Moderate (relies on reading graphics) | **Fast** — named owners, immediately legible |
| Supports booking conversion | Moderate | Good (CTA-forward tone) | **Good** — personal trust drives local-service booking decisions |
| Showcases the team | Incidental | Incidental | **Core to the concept** |
| Works with current real assets | Weakest fit today | Strong fit | **Strong fit** — best-available real photography used at real scale |
| Room for future Fruitbowl photography/video | High ceiling, but starts weak | Moderate (accent-sized slots) | **High ceiling, strong from day one** — direct extension path |
| Desktop + mobile suitability | Needs strong imagery at scale on both | Works well on both | **Especially strong on mobile** — source portraits are already portrait-oriented |

## 6. Recommended Direction: C — "Faces Behind the Fades"

Decisively C, not "it depends." It's the only one of the three that's simultaneously the most
achievable with what's real today, the most aligned with the brief's explicit recognisability bar
(§9: real identity and imagery, not just palette), and the most commercially persuasive to show two
barbers specifically — because it's literally built around them, by name, at full visual weight.
Direction A is the strongest *idea* if a full photography library existed; it doesn't yet, so A is
the right direction for a *later* iteration once a Fruitbowl shoot has happened, not for this
prototype. Direction B is the safest and fastest to build but risks under-delivering on the brief's
own warning against just reproducing the Instagram grid. C avoids both traps.

## 7. Homepage Narrative

Reordered/combined from the brief's content list where it strengthens the flow — not a copy of its
section order.

**1. Hero** — *Purpose:* pass the 15-second test immediately. *Key message:* "This is Faded." *Primary
content:* wordmark + one real image (shopfront, or a cropped portrait) + a short line in Faded's
voice (adapted from "Get faded"). *Visual treatment:* full-bleed grain/monochrome background, boxed
wordmark prominent. *CTA:* primary "Book via Squire," high-contrast, persistent on mobile. *Asset:*
shopfront photo / derived wordmark crop (§9). *Essential.*

**2. Meet the Faces (Team)** — *Purpose:* deliver "understand" fast, and carry Direction C's core
bet. *Key message:* "Run by Elliot & Bailey." *Primary content:* portrait cards in the proven
grey-backdrop, name+title-overlay format. *Visual treatment:* large-scale, grain-consistent,
side-by-side or stacked. *CTA:* none required — this section's job is trust, not conversion.
*Asset:* Elliot/Bailey portrait screenshots (derived, §9). *Essential — highest priority in this
direction.*

**3. The Work** — *Purpose:* visual proof of skill; discovery's most honest current gap. *Key
message:* implicit — "this is what we actually do." *Primary content:* a short strip using existing
work-in-progress crops (from the portrait posts and the "OUR SERVICES" post). *Visual treatment:*
simple grid/strip, same grain treatment. *CTA:* a secondary "Book via Squire" can sit at the end of
this section. *Asset:* today, thin — explicitly the section a future Fruitbowl shoot elevates most.
*Essential as structure; deliberately light as content in v1, and should read as intentional, not
unfinished.*

**4. Services at a Glance** — *Purpose:* inform before click-through. *Key message:* simple, honest
pricing. *Primary content:* the real price list, in the authentic pipe-separated list typography
already found on Instagram. *CTA:* none needed. *Asset:* discovery §2 pricing (Beard Trim price
flagged internally as unconfirmed, §16 — don't publish a number we're not sure of). *Essential.*

**5. Social Proof** — *Purpose:* build trust immediately before the offer/final CTA. *Key message:*
real, honestly-labelled reviews. *Primary content:* Google rating/count and Squire rating/count
shown separately, plus the real quoted review already found ("Best barbers I've been to!...").
*Visual treatment:* styled like Faded's own review-graphic post (stars + pull-quote + grain), not a
generic star widget. *CTA:* none. *Essential.*

**6. FADED25** — *Purpose:* acquisition nudge, placed after trust is established rather than
upfront. *Key message:* single clear offer callout. *Primary content:* code + "via Squire" + neutral
eligibility line (brief §8 — no invented terms). *Visual treatment:* one designed card, not a
banner or popup. *CTA:* this is the second Squire entry point the brief requires (§7) — a natural,
non-arbitrary placement right next to the offer itself. *Essential.*

**7. Instagram Bridge** — *Purpose:* acknowledge Instagram as the deeper content library rather than
pretending to replace it. *Key message:* "more of this on Instagram." *Primary content:* a small
mini-grid echoing real IG tiles, linking out to @fadedselby. *CTA:* outbound link, not a conversion
action. *Asset:* real grid screenshot content, clearly used as inspiration/reference. *Recommended,
not essential — cut first if the page runs long.*

**8. Footer** — *Purpose:* utility. *Key message:* where/when. *Primary content:* address, hours, map
embed, phone. *Visual treatment:* plain, minimal creative risk. *CTA:* a small repeated "Book" link.
*Essential.*

## 8. First 10–15 Second Experience

**Feel** — the grain-textured black/white/grey system, the boxed "FADED." wordmark, and bold
condensed type read as unmistakably Faded before any words are consciously processed.

**Understand** — within the first scroll, the visitor knows this is a real, working Selby
barbershop, run by two named people — not a faceless franchise, not a template.

**Do** — a single, high-contrast "Book via Squire" CTA is visible from the very first screen and
stays reachable throughout (persistent on mobile).

**Trust** — the team section sits early enough (position 2, not buried lower) that real named
people — not just claims or aesthetics — are visible before the page has to lean on any other
proof. This is what stops the page feeling like a visual concept with no substance: it isn't
"trust us," it's "here's exactly who you'd be booking with."

## 10. Typography Direction

**Display type** — bold, condensed/compressed grotesk character; tight tracking at large sizes;
flexible casing (both all-caps and sentence-case are authentically used — preserve that
flexibility rather than forcing one rule).

**Body type** — a clean, neutral, non-condensed sans for anything meant to be read at length
(review quotes, supporting paragraphs) — condensed type is a display device, not a reading
typeface, and should never carry body copy.

**Weight/width** — display: heavy/bold, condensed-to-compressed width. Body: regular/medium,
standard width, generous line-height against dark backgrounds for legibility.

**Hierarchy** — three tiers: (1) display headline, (2) a supporting subhead/line, (3) small tracked
utility text (labels, service-list items, nav) — mirroring the tracked "BARBERS" subtext and the
pipe-separated services list already found.

**Casing** — display flexes per section mood (confirmed authentic to the source material); body
copy stays sentence case for readability; small utility labels stay uppercase and letter-spaced,
matching the existing pattern.

**Spacing** — tight tracking at large display sizes; wider tracking at small utility sizes — the
inverse relationship already visible in the source material.

**Where condensed type should NOT be used** — body paragraphs, review quotes, or any extended
reading text; reserve it for headlines, the wordmark, and short punchy lines only.

**Prototype substitute fonts** (explicitly labelled — not confirmed Faded brand fonts, since no
source typeface file or name was found in discovery): a bold condensed/compressed grotesk such as
**Archivo Black, Anton, or Bebas Neue** for display, paired with a neutral grotesk such as **Inter
or Public Sans** for body/utility text. These are prototyping placeholders only; confirming (or
commissioning) Faded's actual typeface is an open item for build (§16).

## 11. Colour and Texture System

**Primary palette** — off-black (not pure #000) as the dominant background, off-white (not pure
#FFF) as the dominant text colour — softer, more premium contrast that still lets grain texture
read clearly, rather than the harsher flatness of pure black/white.

**Secondary tones** — a small range of mid-greys, drawn from the grey seamless studio backdrop
already used for team portraits, as a secondary surface colour for cards/sections that need to sit
apart from the primary background.

**Contrast rules** — strong off-white-on-off-black for headlines and CTAs (accessibility-checked at
build time); mid-grey reserved for secondary/tertiary text only.

**Grain/noise usage** — the primary, consistent texture device, applied to backgrounds and
photography as atmosphere; kept off small UI controls and body-text areas to protect legibility and
performance.

**Secondary texture** — the rough brush/paint-textured device (seen on the "IT'S COMING HOME" tile)
used sparingly as an occasional accent (e.g. a section divider or the offer card), never mixed
into the same element as the grain treatment.

**Image treatment** — every photograph, regardless of source colour, graded to the same
monochrome/grain system for consistency — including any temporarily-sourced placeholder imagery.

**Pure black/white vs. off-black/off-white** — off-black/off-white dominant throughout the page;
pure black reserved specifically for the wordmark treatment, matching how Faded already presents it.

**Avoiding flatness** — grain plus subtle grey gradients/vignettes (as seen in the real backdrop
photography) plus occasional brush-texture accents, rather than large flat solid-colour blocks.

## 12. Motion / Interaction Guidance

Used only where it earns its place against the brand, not for novelty:
- Subtle, slow-moving grain animation as hero background atmosphere.
- A grain-to-clear crossfade as team/work images enter the viewport on scroll — thematically apt
  for a brand called "Faded," and functional (draws the eye to the team section specifically).
- Gentle hover states on team cards (slight scale or grain shift) — desktop only, not required for
  the mobile experience.
- A persistent, thumb-reachable mobile booking CTA.

**Explicitly avoided:** parallax scrolling, scroll-jacking, or elaborate showreel-style transitions
— these read as generic agency portfolio tropes, not as anything evidenced in Faded's own brand,
and risk working against the "real Selby barbershop" feel the brief protects.

## 13. Mobile Behaviour

Treated as first-class, not a compressed afterthought — appropriate given a social-referral
business:
- Hero and team cards use portrait-orientation crops, matching the real source photography
  directly rather than requiring awkward re-cropping.
- The primary booking CTA is persistent and thumb-reachable (bottom of screen), not just present in
  a hero that scrolls away.
- Team cards behave as simple stacked/swipeable cards, echoing the familiarity of an Instagram
  carousel.
- Pricing is a simple, scannable vertical list — never a wide table requiring horizontal scroll.
- FADED25 is a single clear card, not a banner that pushes page content down excessively.
- The Squire handoff is clearly signposted ("Book via Squire") so leaving the site to book doesn't
  feel like an error or a broken link.
- Overall page length stays disciplined — a homepage completable in a reasonable number of swipes,
  not an endless scroll.

## 14. Second-Page Recommendation

**No second page for this prototype.** The homepage-led MVP can do the job on its own: with only
two real, high-quality team portraits and a handful of other real images available today, a
separate "Team" or "Gallery" page would either thinly duplicate content already on the homepage or
need placeholder padding to feel complete — working against the real-assets-first principle rather
than serving it (brief §9). The clear, explicit trigger to revisit this: once a future Fruitbowl
shoot produces enough real team and work photography (roughly 4–5+ full portraits, a real
finished-cut library) to justify a dedicated page rather than stretching the homepage's single Work
section thin.

## 15. Risks / Things to Avoid

Explicit guardrails against generic design-agency tropes, checked against the recommended
direction specifically:
- **No generic beige "premium" styling** — the palette stays Faded's own off-black/off-white/grey
  system, never a warm neutral agency palette.
- **No generic barber stock photography** — real Faded assets first, labelled placeholders only
  where genuinely necessary (§9), never presented as if real.
- **No over-designed animation** — §12 explicitly rules out parallax/scroll-jacking novelty.
- **No glossy luxury-grooming clichés** — no marble, gold accents, or spa-language; the tone stays
  Faded's own confident-and-local voice, not aspirational luxury.
- **No fake heritage/vintage barbershop aesthetics** — no invented "est. 1950s" styling; Faded is a
  young shop (2024) and the brand is contemporary/streetwear-adjacent, not traditional.
- **No overly corporate layout** — team-led, personal framing throughout; never generic "our team"
  boilerplate.
- **Not a copy of Blak Bear** — the pattern borrowed is structural (owned site + Squire booking,
  team-led content), never Blak Bear's specific visual identity or multi-site scale positioning
  (discovery §6).
- **Not a fashion brand that happens to cut hair** — the streetwear/editorial visual cues stay
  grounded by real local specificity (named owners, real reviews, real Selby address) rather than
  drifting into disconnected aspirational styling.

## 16. Open Items and Prototype Handling

Most of the items below are not blockers to starting the prototype build. This speculative
prototype can proceed by handling them provisionally, clearly labelled as such internally. A
smaller set of items genuinely must be resolved before any real client-facing live launch.

### Safe to handle provisionally in the speculative prototype

- **No official high-resolution/vector logo available**: use a faithful temporary
  prototype-derived wordmark if needed; clearly label it internally as derived from Faded's public
  visual material; do not present it as an official source logo file.
- **Official Faded typeface unknown**: use the approved prototype substitute fonts (§10); keep
  them clearly documented as substitutes, not official brand fonts.
- **Limited photography**: use the real Faded imagery currently available; use screenshot-derived
  assets where appropriate for this speculative prototype; preserve provenance internally; design
  the layout so higher-quality Fruitbowl-shot assets can replace them later without structural
  redesign (§9).
- **FADED25 eligibility unclear**: keep offer wording neutral; do not state new-client-only or
  new-and-existing-client eligibility as fact.
- **Beard Trim price unresolved**: omit the disputed Beard Trim price (£10 vs £15, discovery §2)
  from the prototype rather than guessing.

### Must be confirmed before any real client-facing live launch

- Official logo/source assets.
- Official typography/brand files, if available.
- Permission/final asset handover for production use.
- Final current team roster.
- FADED25's actual current eligibility terms.
- Accurate current service pricing.
- Any higher-resolution photography/video required for production quality.
