# Operation Faded — Creative + Commercial Brief

Prepared by Fruitbowl. This is a decision document, not a design or build artefact. It translates
`docs/discovery.md` into the specific calls needed before any visual or technical work starts.

## 1. Purpose of This Phase

Discovery established what Faded already is. This brief decides what the prototype is *for*, what
it must contain, and what it must deliberately leave out — so the next phase (design/build) has
firm ground to stand on rather than open questions.

## 2. What the Prototype Needs to Prove

To Elliot and Bailey specifically:
- That we understood their actual business, not a generic barbershop — the brand we show them
  should be recognisably an elevated version of what they already built, not a Fruitbowl-branded
  replacement of it.
- That a proper digital front door doesn't mean disrupting what already works — Squire stays,
  Instagram stays, nothing they rely on today gets threatened or replaced.
- That there's a credible, concrete gap between what they have now (a generic booking-platform
  page) and what's possible — made obvious by direct comparison, not asserted in a pitch deck.

To Fruitbowl, commercially: that "social-first brand discovery → elevated prototype" is a real,
repeatable, sellable methodology — this is the first proof case, not a one-off favour.

## 3. Success Criteria

The prototype succeeds if, shown to Elliot and Bailey cold:
1. They recognise it as *Faded* within seconds, unprompted — because it uses their real identity
   and imagery, not just a matching colour palette (see §9, Asset Strategy).
2. They perceive it as more credible/premium than their current Squire page, also unprompted.
3. They can immediately see how a real customer would get from landing to booking.
4. Nothing in it makes them feel their existing brand, tone, or team was overridden or ignored.

If a viewer can't tell within 15 seconds whether this is Faded's site or a generic template with
their logo dropped in, the prototype has failed regardless of visual polish.

## 4. The First 10–15 Seconds

This is the single most important design constraint, made explicit so it can't get lost later:

- **Feel**: unmistakably Faded — the grain-textured black/white/grey system, the boxed FADED
  wordmark, bold condensed type — not a soft, neutral "premium local business" palette.
- **Understand**: this is a real, working Selby barbershop, run by two named people (Elliot and
  Bailey), not a faceless franchise or a stock-photo template.
- **Do**: see a clear, unambiguous path to book, presented in Faded's own voice — not a generic
  "Book Now" button styled like every other service-business site.

Booking is the primary *action* the page drives toward, but the page's overall *purpose* is wider
than that single click: elevate the brand, build trust, and strengthen consideration, so that by
the time someone reaches a booking CTA they're already convinced — not just funnelled at speed.
Everything on the homepage is subordinate to the feel/understand/do test, but "do" doesn't mean
rushing past the brand and trust work to get there.

## 5. Homepage-Led Minimum Viable Prototype — What's In

The homepage carries the core experience and is the clear priority — but this is not a hard rule
against anything beyond it. If visual/design exploration later surfaces a genuine reason for a
lightweight second page (for example, a deeper team page or a fuller services view), that's a
reasonable extension of this brief, not a violation of it. What stays fixed regardless of page
count: Squire owns actual booking, and the homepage must stand alone as a complete, convincing
experience even if nothing else is ever built.

Homepage sections, in order of priority against the feel/understand/do test:

1. **Hero** — the FADED wordmark treatment, grain/monochrome system, a short, confident line in
   Faded's own voice (adapted from "Get faded" / their existing tone, not invented from scratch),
   and the primary booking CTA pointing straight at Squire.
2. **Meet the faces behind the fades** — Elliot and Bailey, using the exact framing already proven
   in their own content (discovery §7), not generic "our team" copy. This does double duty: it's
   the strongest existing brand asset *and* the fastest way to make the "understand" test land.
3. **Services at a glance** — the existing simple price list, presented cleanly; not the whole
   Squire booking flow re-implemented, just enough to inform before the click-through.
4. **Social proof, shown honestly** — reviews presented separately by platform, exactly as
   discovery §11 specifies (never a fabricated combined number).
5. **FADED25** — one clear, well-designed placement (see §8), not repeated throughout the page.
6. **A bridge to Instagram** — real content lives there; the homepage should point to it rather
   than pretend to replace it, given discovery found the account itself is the stronger content
   source available right now.
7. **Footer** — address, hours, map, phone — plain utility, no creative risk needed here.

**What's deliberately out of scope for this prototype**: a full booking system (Squire's job), a
blog/content section, individual full profile pages for every barber, e-commerce/products, and
anything requiring ongoing content the client would need to maintain immediately. A second
lightweight page is a permitted extension if justified during design; a full multi-page site
rebuild is not what this phase is scoping.

## 6. How Much Instagram Language Transfers

Substantially, but adapted, not copy-pasted:
- Reuse proven, real phrases as load-bearing copy: "Get faded," "Meet the faces behind the fades."
- The "Step away from the noise / sit back, switch off" theme (discovery flagged as an *emerging*
  theme, not a confirmed pillar) is worth testing here as a secondary supporting line, precisely
  because a homepage is a lower-risk place to try it than committing it to the core brand — if it
  doesn't earn its place, it's easy to cut later.
- Punchier meme-format devices (the "Do Not Disturb" graphic, the "Weekend is loading" spinner) are
  Instagram-native and shouldn't be forced onto a homepage wholesale — the *tone* they represent
  (playful, confident, internet-literate) should carry over; the specific formats don't need to.
- Culturally-topical, time-bound content (the football pun) belongs to social, not a homepage that
  needs to still make sense in a year — noted as a difference in what each channel is *for*, not a
  quality judgement.

## 7. Where Squire Enters the Journey

The homepage **never** attempts to replace Squire's booking flow. Two entry points only —
1. Primary CTA in the hero (the single most important click on the page).
2. A secondary CTA near the team/services section, for anyone who scrolls before deciding.

Both point at the same real Squire link identified in discovery
(`getsquire.com/booking/book/faded-barbers-selby-selby`). No parallel form, no duplicated admin,
no third booking surface invented for the prototype — even if a second page is later added (§5),
it still funnels into this same single Squire link, not a new booking surface.

## 8. FADED25 — Prominence Without Choosing Faded's Terms For Them

Shown once, prominently and cleanly — not nagging, not repeated in three sections. Discovery found
genuinely conflicting eligibility wording across Faded's own posts (§11), and this brief does
**not** resolve that conflict on Faded's behalf. Instead:
- The prototype demonstrates the *mechanism* — a single code (FADED25), redeemed through Squire, in
  one well-designed placement — without asserting specific eligibility terms (new clients only vs.
  new-and-existing) as settled fact.
- Copy stays neutral/placeholder where eligibility would otherwise need stating (e.g. "Ask in-shop
  or check current terms on booking" rather than inventing a definitive rule), or is visibly marked
  as a placeholder if a concrete example is needed to demonstrate the layout.
- An explicit statement travels with the prototype into any handover: **final FADED25 eligibility
  terms must be confirmed directly with Faded before this — or any offer messaging — goes live
  publicly.**

## 9. Prototype Asset Strategy — Real Faded Assets First

Recognition has to come from Faded's actual identity and imagery, not just a reproduced
black-and-white palette — that's the difference between "this looks like a nice barber site" and
"this is unmistakably Faded." Priority order for populating the prototype:
1. **Faded's actual FADED logo/wordmark**, as seen in their own content — not a redrawn or
   reinterpreted version.
2. **Real photography of the shop/shopfront/interior** where available from public sources
   (including, with care, material already gathered in `docs/research/instagram/` during
   discovery).
3. **Real team and barber photography** — the existing co-owner portrait style is a strong,
   reusable asset in its own right.
4. **Other imagery already published publicly by Faded** (Instagram grid content, graphics,
   review-quote posts) reused or adapted where it fits the homepage's structure.
5. **Only where a genuine content gap remains**, a clearly-labelled placeholder — internally marked
   as a placeholder, never presented as if it were Faded's real content, and never generic barber
   stock photography unless there is truly no better option.

This is deliberately a "use what exists now, design for what's next" strategy: the prototype should
be built using Faded's own real assets wherever they exist today, but structured with strong,
deliberate placements for photography/video — hero imagery, team portraits, work-in-progress
shots — so that if Fruitbowl later shoots original photography/video for Faded, it drops into a
homepage already designed to be elevated by it, without needing to be rebuilt. The photography/
video gap discovery identified (§12 of discovery) is treated as a *future* Fruitbowl-delivered
opportunity, not something the speculative prototype needs to fully solve today.

## 10. What "Impressive Enough to Show Them" Actually Means

Not: maximal visual polish, novel interaction design, or features Faded didn't ask for. It means:
the specific combination of *instant recognisability via real identity and imagery* ("that's us")
and *visible elevation* ("...but this is so much better than what we have"). A beautiful site that
doesn't pass the recognisability test has failed this brief even if it would impress a design
audience — the audience here is two barbers looking at their own business, not a portfolio review.

Concretely, the bar is: if you covered the Faded name/logo and Elliot/Bailey couldn't tell within
seconds it was their shop from the real imagery, visual system, and verbal language alone, it isn't
ready.

## 11. Commercial Framing

This prototype is speculative work product, not a delivered client project — Faded has not been
engaged. Its commercial purpose is twofold: (1) a concrete "here's what your business could look
like online" conversation-starter for Faded specifically, and (2) the first proof case for
Fruitbowl's repeatable "social-first discovery → elevated prototype" service (discovery §8/§14). No
pricing, contracts, or commitments are implied by the prototype's existence. Next steps after any
positive reaction from Faded (a real brief, a scoped engagement, pricing) are explicitly out of
scope for this document.

## 12. Open Risks / Watch-Outs Going Into Design

- Real photography/video is still limited (discovery §12) — §9's asset strategy governs how the
  prototype handles this; the risk is defaulting to generic stock out of convenience instead of
  following that priority order.
- The FADED25 neutral-terms decision (§8) must not quietly turn into an invented "final" version
  during design/build — it stays a placeholder until Faded confirms.
- Scope discipline: the temptation to "just add one more page" now that a second page is permitted
  (§5) is real — any addition still has to justify itself against the feel/understand/do test, not
  just "it would be nice to have."
